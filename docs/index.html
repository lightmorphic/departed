Departed

If you stop answering,
one person receives
what you left them.

A small, patient program. Every ten days it asks whether you are still here. You click once and it goes back to waiting. It runs on a computer in your own home and asks nothing of anybody else.

See how it works Set it up

You choose one person.

You lock the files yourself, before they ever reach it.

The key is given by hand, and never to a machine.

Twenty-one quiet days

Nothing happens quickly here. It is built to be slow, so that an ordinary week away is never mistaken for something else.

  1. Day ten

    An email arrives with one link in it.

    It asks nothing of you but a click. The clock returns to the beginning, a note comes back to say it was heard, and the link is retired so it can never be used twice.

  2. Days eleven to twenty

    If you don't click, it asks again.

    The same gentle email, once a day, ten more times. Any one of them will do. A holiday, a hospital stay, a phone left in a drawer — there is a great deal of room here for ordinary life.

  3. Day twenty-one

    It sends what you prepared.

    One email, to the one person you named, with your locked files attached exactly as you left them. You are told as well, in case you are simply somewhere without a signal.

  4. Afterwards

    It stops.

    It does not send again, or keep asking, or tell anybody else. It has done the only thing it was ever made to do.

What it never knows

You lock the files on your own computer, with a passphrase of your own choosing. Only then do they go into the folder it watches. It never looks inside, never holds the key, never writes it down anywhere — not in its notes, not in its settings, not in its records of the day.

It is a courier carrying a sealed envelope. If somebody stole the machine tomorrow, or read the email on its way, they would have the sealed envelope and nothing else.

The person you chose already has the key. You gave it to them yourself, out loud, in a room.

If something goes wrong

Most of the work in a thing like this is what it does on a bad day.

If it sends by mistake

You are emailed too, the moment it happens. Change the passphrase, click once, and it begins again from the start. A false alarm costs you an afternoon, not your privacy.

If there is nothing to send

It will not send an empty envelope. If the folder is bare when the day comes, it holds everything and writes to you instead, once a day, until there is something there.

If the message won't go

A mail server that is down does not end the matter. It tries again every few minutes, for as long as it takes, and it never records the thing as done until it truly is.

If the machine was switched off

It does not come back and act on a stale clock. It picks up where it left off and works through the whole run of asking, one day at a time, before it does anything final.

It lives in your house

Departed runs on a small computer of your own, on your own network, reachable only by you. There is no company holding your files, no account to lose, nothing to keep paying for. Nobody can be asked to hand over what they were never given.

It is free, and the whole of it is open to read, so that anyone you trust can check for themselves what it does and what it cannot do.

Setting it up

This part needs somebody comfortable with a home server — you, or whoever looks after yours. It is three commands and a settings file.

docker compose up -d

You put your locked files in one folder, write your email address, your person's email address and your mail settings into a plain settings file, and open the page it serves to press Send a test email. That test posts the whole thing to you rather than to them, so you can watch it arrive before you ever rely on it.
